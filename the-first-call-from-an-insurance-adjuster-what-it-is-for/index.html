The First Call From an Insurance Adjuster: What It Is For

Marcus Ross · 2026-11-04

After a crash, the first call from an insurance adjuster usually comes within a day or two. It feels routine, and it is supposed to. It is also where a lot of a claim's shape gets set, so it helps to know what the call is for before the phone rings.

Who is calling

If you are claiming against your own policy, the adjuster works for your insurer and you have a contract with them. If you are claiming against the other driver's policy, the adjuster works for that company. Both are doing a job, but the second one has no contract with you, and their company pays less when your claim is smaller. Ask which company they work for and write down their name, phone number and the claim number.

What they usually want on the first call

  • Basic facts: date, time, place, the cars involved and who was driving.
  • Where your car is now, so an appraiser can look at it.
  • Whether anyone was hurt.
  • Often, a recorded statement about how the crash happened.

Things worth doing

  • Give the plain facts you are sure of. "I don't know" is a complete answer when you don't.
  • Keep injury talk to what has been diagnosed so far. Soreness that shows up the next day is common, and "I'm fine" on day one gets quoted back later.
  • Ask what the next step is and when to expect the appraisal.
  • Follow up the call with a short email that lists what was discussed, so there is a written record.

Things you can decline or postpone

With the other driver's insurer, you are generally not required to give a recorded statement, and you can ask to do it later or in writing. Your own policy is different: most policies include a duty to cooperate, which can include a statement, so read that section before you say no. You also do not have to sign a medical records release on the first call; a broad release can cover years of unrelated history.

Keep a claim file

One folder, paper or digital, with the claim numbers, photos, repair estimates, rental receipts, medical bills and every letter or email. Claims run for weeks or months, and the person with the organized file has the easier time.
